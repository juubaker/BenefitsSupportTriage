# Medical Plan Eligibility

## Who is eligible {#chunk-elig-01}

Full-time employees working 30 or more hours per week are eligible for medical,
dental, and vision coverage on the first of the month following their hire date.
Part-time employees working fewer than 20 hours per week are not eligible for
medical coverage but may be eligible for voluntary benefits.

## Adding dependents {#chunk-dep-02}

Eligible dependents include a legal spouse, a domestic partner, and children up
to age 26. Children may remain on the plan until the end of the month in which
they turn 26. Documentation (marriage certificate, birth certificate, or proof
of domestic partnership) is required within 31 days of the qualifying event.

# Open Enrollment

## Enrollment window {#chunk-oe-01}

Open enrollment runs for two weeks each fall. Elections made during open
enrollment take effect on January 1 of the following year. Outside of open
enrollment, changes are only permitted within 31 days of a qualifying life
event.

## Qualifying life events {#chunk-qle-12}

Qualifying life events include marriage, divorce, birth or adoption of a child,
loss of other coverage, and a change in employment status. An employee has 31
days from the date of the event to make a corresponding change to their
elections.

# Health Savings Account (HSA)

## Eligibility and limits {#chunk-hsa-22}

An HSA is available only to employees enrolled in a qualifying high-deductible
health plan (HDHP). Employees enrolled in any other medical plan, or who are
enrolled in Medicare, are not eligible to contribute to an HSA. Annual
contribution limits are set by the IRS and may change each year.

## HSA contribution limits for 2026 {#chunk-hsa-limits-2026}

For calendar year 2026, the IRS annual HSA contribution limit is $4,400 for
self-only HDHP coverage and $8,750 for family HDHP coverage. Employees age 55
or older may contribute an additional $1,000 catch-up contribution. The limit
includes both employee and employer contributions. For 2026, a qualifying
family HDHP must have a minimum deductible of $3,400 and an out-of-pocket
maximum no greater than $17,000.

# Dependent Care FSA

## Dependent care FSA contribution limits for 2026 {#chunk-dcfsa-2026}

Beginning in 2026, the annual dependent care FSA contribution limit is $7,500
per household ($3,750 for married employees filing separately), up from $5,000
in prior years. A dependent care FSA is separate from an HSA and reimburses
eligible child or adult day care expenses, not medical expenses.
# Medical Plans

## Changing your primary care physician {#chunk-pcp-01}

HMO members must designate a primary care physician (PCP) for each covered
family member. You may change your PCP at any time through the member portal or
by calling the number on your ID card. A change requested on or before the 15th
of the month takes effect the first of the following month; a change requested
after the 15th takes effect the first of the month after that. There is no limit
on how often you may change your PCP, and no qualifying life event is required.

## HMO service area and network {#chunk-hmo-network-02}

The HMO uses a regional network and provides no coverage for care received from
non-network providers, except for emergency care and urgent care while
travelling. Members who move outside the plan's service area must change to the
PPO; a permanent move out of the service area is a qualifying life event that
permits a mid-year plan change within 31 days of the move. Care received from a
non-network provider inside the service area is the member's responsibility in
full.

## PPO out-of-network coverage {#chunk-ppo-06}

The PPO covers care from both network and non-network providers. Network care is
covered at 90% after the deductible; non-network care is covered at 60% of the
allowed amount after a separate non-network deductible, and the provider may bill
the member for charges above the allowed amount. PPO premiums are higher than
HMO premiums for the same coverage tier, and the difference is published in the
annual rate sheet issued before open enrollment.

## Prescription drug tiers {#chunk-rx-03}

Prescriptions are covered in four tiers. Tier 1 (generic) has a $10 copay for a
30-day supply, tier 2 (preferred brand) $35, tier 3 (non-preferred brand) $70,
and tier 4 (specialty) 20% coinsurance up to $250 per fill. A 90-day supply
through mail order is dispensed for two copays. Specialty medications must be
filled through the designated specialty pharmacy.

# Dental and Vision

## Dental plan benefits {#chunk-dental-03}

The dental PPO pays 100% for preventive care, 80% for basic restorative care,
and 50% for major services, after a $50 individual deductible. The annual
maximum benefit is $2,000 per covered person per calendar year; amounts above
the annual maximum are the member's responsibility. Orthodontia for dependent
children has a separate $1,500 lifetime maximum.

## Vision plan benefits {#chunk-vision-02}

The vision plan covers one routine eye exam every 12 months with a $10 copay.
Lenses are covered once every 12 months and frames once every 24 months, with a
$150 frame allowance. Contact lenses may be elected in place of lenses and
frames, with a $150 annual allowance.

# Continuation and Coordination of Coverage

## COBRA continuation coverage {#chunk-cobra-04}

An employee or dependent who loses coverage because of termination, a reduction
in hours, divorce, or a child reaching age 26 may continue the same coverage
under COBRA for up to 18 months, or 36 months for dependents in the case of
divorce or loss of dependent status. The election period is 60 days from the
later of the coverage end date or the notice date. The member pays the full
premium plus a 2% administrative fee, so COBRA typically costs substantially
more than coverage as an active employee or as a dependent on a spouse's plan.

## Coordination of benefits {#chunk-coord-01}

A person may be covered by more than one medical plan, for example through their
own employer and a spouse's. The plan covering the person as an employee pays
first, and the other plan may cover remaining eligible charges. Enrollment in a
non-HDHP second plan, including as a dependent, ends HSA contribution
eligibility for the months that coverage is in effect. Members must report other
coverage within 31 days of its start.

## Documentation requirements for mid-year changes {#chunk-docs-31}

Every mid-year election change must be supported by documentation submitted
within 31 days of the event: a marriage certificate for marriage, a divorce
decree for divorce, a birth certificate or placement order for birth or
adoption, and a letter from the prior carrier or employer showing the coverage
end date for loss of other coverage. A move out of a plan's service area
requires proof of the new address. Changes requested without documentation are
not processed, and the 31-day window is not extended while documents are
gathered.

# Spending Accounts

## Health care FSA {#chunk-fsa-07}

The health care FSA allows pre-tax contributions up to the IRS annual limit for
eligible medical, dental, and vision expenses. Up to $660 of unused funds rolls
over to the following plan year; amounts above the rollover limit are forfeited.
Unlike an HSA, the full annual election is available on the first day of the
plan year, the account is not portable when employment ends, and an employee may
not contribute to both a health care FSA and an HSA in the same period.

## Commuter benefits {#chunk-commuter-01}

Employees may set aside pre-tax dollars for work-related transit and parking
expenses, each up to the IRS monthly limit of $340 for 2026. Elections may be
started, changed, or stopped monthly and do not require a qualifying life event.
Unused balances carry forward while employed but are forfeited at termination.

# Income Protection

## Basic and supplemental life insurance {#chunk-life-02}

The company provides basic life insurance equal to one times annual base salary,
up to $500,000, at no cost to the employee. Employees may elect supplemental
coverage of one to five times salary, and spouse or child coverage, during open
enrollment or within 31 days of a qualifying life event. Supplemental elections
above the guaranteed issue amount require evidence of insurability.

## Short-term disability {#chunk-std-04}

Short-term disability replaces 60% of base pay for up to 26 weeks following a
7-calendar-day waiting period, which must be satisfied with accrued sick time or
unpaid leave. Benefits require certification from a treating provider and begin
on day 8 of the approved absence. Long-term disability may begin when short-term
disability is exhausted.

## Leaves of absence {#chunk-leave-03}

Eligible employees may take up to 12 weeks of job-protected leave per year for
their own serious health condition, to care for a family member, or for the
birth, adoption, or foster placement of a child. Parental leave of 8 weeks is
paid at 100% of base pay and may run concurrently with other leave. Medical,
dental, and vision coverage continue during approved leave on the same terms,
and FSA contributions are suspended during unpaid leave and resume at the prior
election on return unless a change is requested within 31 days.

## Maternity and prenatal coverage {#chunk-maternity-02}

Prenatal visits, routine screenings, and delivery are covered as medical
benefits under all plans; prenatal visits and most screenings are covered at
100% as preventive care in network. Birth is a qualifying life event, and the
newborn may be added within 31 days of the date of birth with coverage effective
from that date. Short-term disability is payable for the recovery period
following delivery, typically 6 weeks for an uncomplicated vaginal delivery and
8 weeks following a cesarean section, and runs concurrently with paid parental
leave.

# Retirement and Wellbeing

## 401(k) plan and company match {#chunk-401k-05}

The company matches 100% of the first 3% of eligible pay contributed and 50% of
the next 2%, for a maximum match of 4% of eligible pay. Employees are
automatically enrolled at 3% after 30 days of employment and may change or stop
contributions at any time. Employee contributions are always fully vested;
company match contributions vest after two years of service.

## New hire enrollment {#chunk-newhire-01}

New hires have 31 days from their hire date to elect medical, dental, and vision
coverage, with coverage effective the first of the month following the hire
date. An employee who does not elect within 31 days must wait for open
enrollment or a qualifying life event. 401(k) participation begins after 30 days
of employment and is separate from the medical enrollment window.

## Employee assistance program {#chunk-eap-01}

The employee assistance program (EAP) provides up to 8 free counseling sessions
per issue per year for employees and household members, with no copay and no
enrollment required. The EAP also offers legal consultations, financial
coaching, and referrals for childcare and eldercare. Use of the program is
confidential and is not reported to the employer.
